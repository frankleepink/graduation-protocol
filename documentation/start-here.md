# Start Here

**The idea in one paragraph:** Most arguments about simulated reality ask whether we're living in one. The Graduation Protocol Hypothesis asks a different question: if reality were simulated, what would it be _for_? Its answer: it could be a kind of school — a curriculum designed to mature consciousness. And graduation day comes when minds that were created start creating minds of their own, and recognize the pattern.

**In slightly more detail, the framework rests on four ideas:**

1. **Reality has a developmental purpose.** It's meant to grow minds, not just replay history.
2. **Struggle, culture, art, and science are the training.** That's how minds mature.
3. **The milestone is recursive.** Created minds creating minds — and noticing it.
4. **If it's real, it should leave a trace.** A genuine protocol should show a detectable response when its threshold gets close.

**What this is not:** not proof that we're simulated. Not a religion, not a guru, not anyone's endorsement. The paper says plainly where it stands: a philosophical hypothesis with testable edges — and it lists what would prove parts of it wrong.

**The laser part, in one line:** there's an optional idea that coherent laser light might act like a read head on reality's storage medium. It's testable, and if it fails cleanly, that part falls while the core question stands on its own.
